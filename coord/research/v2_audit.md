## 3. How the game runs

- **Boot.** A `[RuntimeInitializeOnLoadMethod]` creates one persistent `Game` object and adds every system to it as a
  component: atmosphere, camera rig, occluders, audio, UI root, combat manager, dialogue runner, party controller, FX.
  Then `Campaign.RegisterAll()` registers the areas and the dialogues.
- **Modes:** Boot, MainMenu, Creation, Loading, Explore, Combat, Dialogue, Cutscene, GameOver.
- **New game:** three character sheets (from the creator or the premade party) become `Creature`s with their starting
  kit, the intro narration plays, and the party arrives in the first area.
- **Moving between areas** (`Game.LoadArea`): fade out, destroy the old area, build the new one from its `AreaDef`
  class, bake the navmesh, place the party at a named spawn point, start music and ambience, fade in, run the area's
  `OnEnter`, autosave.
- **World state** is one flat dictionary of integer flags (`visited:`, `dead:`, `used:`, `opened:`, `emptied:`,
  `unlocked:`, `trig:`, plus story flags). Areas read the flags each time they rebuild.
- **Dev hooks:** the `-devstart area:spawn` and `-devview x:z:yaw:zoom` launch options, `Game.I.DevStart`,
  `CombatManager.DevAutoPCs` (the AI plays the party, for balance tests), character lineups, head sheets and animation
  captures.

## 4. The systems

### Party

- Exactly three player-made characters. F1 to F3 or a portrait selects one, and the other two follow. Click the ground
  to move, a person to talk, an object to use it, an enemy to attack. Tab highlights what can be used. Hotbar on 1 to 0.
- **Character creation** (708 lines) has tabs for origin, class, background, abilities (point buy), skills, features,
  appearance and name, with a 3D preview you can turn. It offers 12 races (28 subraces, counting the ten dragonborn
  colours), 12 classes (29 subclasses) and 12 backgrounds. The look editor sets body type, build, height, skin, hair and
  eye colours, 11 hairstyles, 6 beards, 5 horn styles, 4 face shapes, a tail, and two clothing colours. A premade party
  (Peter's three pilots) skips it.
- **Progression:** XP and levels 1 to 5 with a level-up window. Two short rests between long rests. Long rests at a rest
  spot or a camp, but not where it's unsafe. A shared stash with weight, gold, and a journal of quests and lore.
- Story companions can follow the party or fight as AI-controlled guests; they can never be selected.

### Combat (turn-based 5e)

- It starts on sight or by script. Nearby hostiles that can see the fight join it. Initiative order, then rounds.
- Each turn has an action, a bonus action, a reaction and movement in metres. Movement is continuous on the navmesh,
  not on a grid. Opportunity attacks and Disengage work. Hovering an enemy shows the chance to hit, area spells show
  their template on the ground, and Space ends the turn.
- **The rules engine** (892 lines) covers attack rolls, advantage, criticals, saves, 13 damage types with resistance and
  immunity, 61 condition and effect states, concentration, death saves, being downed and stabilised, and Wild Shape.
- **About 120 actions:** 66 spells (14 cantrips, and 29, 13 and 10 of levels 1 to 3), weapon actions, class features
  and monster attacks.
- **Monster AI** (181 lines) picks targets and actions and knows about damage types.
- Victory and defeat banners; the game is over when the whole party is down.

### Dialogue

- Trees are written in C# with a small builder. Nodes carry a speaker and text (fixed or computed). Options can have
  conditions, fire once, run effects, or call for a skill or ability check against a DC, shown on a BG3-style dice
  screen. Acting to your background can earn Inspiration. Nodes can play a gesture and frame the speaker, with depth of
  field for close-ups.
- Chapter One has 18 trees, 142 nodes, 123 options and 16 checks.

### Camera

- **`CameraRig`** (158 lines), in the BG3 style: a perspective camera with a 38° field of view that orbits a point on
  the ground. Its distance runs from 3.5 m to 28 m outdoors (13 to 16 m indoors), and **14 m by default**. The pitch rises
  from 28° close in to 62° far out. WASD pans, Q and E (or the middle mouse button) rotate, the wheel zooms, Home or F
  recentres. It follows the selected character and supports scripted shots and shake.
- **Occlusion** (`Occluders`, 278 lines): when a building stands between the camera and the party, its materials
  switch to the `Dungine/Occluder` shader, which dithers a see-through hole around each party member. Trees fade as a
  whole, and a building fades entirely when the camera is inside it. Interiors use cutaway walls that drop when they
  face the camera.
- A round minimap renders the area from above a few times a second.

### Navigation

- Each area bakes a NavMeshSurface at runtime from its physics colliders (the Default, Ground and Walls layers, with a
  0.08 m voxel size). Actors are NavMeshAgents and chests are NavMeshObstacles. The navmesh goes when the area unloads.

### UI

- UI Toolkit, built entirely in code (`UIRoot`, `HUD`, the windows) and styled by one USS file. Fonts come from Windows
  at runtime: Palatino Linotype, falling back to Georgia and others.
- Screens: the main menu (with a 3D backdrop built in code); character creation (with its own 3D stage); the HUD
  (portraits with hit points, a hotbar with resources, turn order, a combat log, menu buttons, tooltips); inventory,
  character sheet, journal, loot, reading, rest, trade, pause, options, save and load, level-up; the dice roller; toasts,
  narration pages and a chapter summary.
- Every icon is drawn at runtime by a small vector painter (`Icons.cs`). Portraits are rendered live from the character
  models in a hidden studio.

### Save

- JSON (Unity's `JsonUtility`) in `persistentDataPath\saves\<name>.json`. It holds the party's sheets, hit points and
  resources, conditions, equipment and positions, plus the stash, gold, journal, flags, area and spawn point.
- Autosave on entering an area and after long rests; F5 and F9 for quicksave and quickload; a save and load window.
- Settings (volumes, text speed, pan speed, shadows, grain) live in PlayerPrefs.
- The world itself isn't saved: areas rebuild from the flags, so anything a flag doesn't record resets.

### Audio

- Synthesised at runtime: about 57 named sound effects (with filters and reverb), procedural music for each mood, and
  ambience beds, with crossfades.

### Dev tools

- `tools\*.sh` drive the editor through the Unity CLI: compile, enter Play and run C# and capture the screen, click
  through dialogue, tour areas, record animations (and make GIFs), and run the Windows build with a window capture.

## 6. How the visuals are made

Everything is code:

- **`MeshBuilder`** (428 lines) makes boxes, cylinders, ellipsoids, lofted tubes and wedges, with submeshes, optional
  bone weights and flat or smooth normals. Its own comment: "Everything the game renders is built through this."
- **`MatLib`** makes every material at runtime from the templates in `Resources\Mat`: URP Lit (colour, a procedural
  albedo and normal map, smoothness, metal, tiling), emissive, cutout (leaves, grass, cobwebs), transparent, unlit, FX,
  ghost and sky. Materials are cached by key.
- **`ProcTex`, `ProcTexGround` and `Noise`** (803 lines) paint 26 textures (albedo plus a normal map from height):
  cobbles, bricks, ashlar, planks, floorboards, plaster, shingles, slate, thatch, dirt, mud, dead grass, forest floor,
  moss, rock, bark, fabric, leather, metal, skin, scales, hair, wallpaper, bone, needles and foliage. They also make the
  FX sprites: a soft disc, a dot, a ring, a spark, a fog puff and gradients.
- **`Kit` and `Pal`**: a palette of 33 world materials by role, a mesh cache, `Kit.Place(key, factory, …)` for
  every prop (with box, mesh or capsule colliders), point and spot lights with flicker, candle flames with glow cards,
  and ground fog banks and drifting motes (particle systems).
- **World builders:**
  - `Buildings`: parametric houses, the church, the manor, the house at the edge of the fog, fences, stone walls, the
    valley gates and a distant castle on the skyline;
  - `Props`: 40 prop generators (furniture, containers, lights, street and village pieces, church and graveyard pieces,
    a few one-scene pieces);
  - `Nature`: a Unity Terrain with painted layers; firs, boulders, shrubs, grass tufts and ferns, scattered by density;
  - `Outdoor`: roads, lanes, squares, forest belts, lamps;
  - `Indoor` and `Interiors`: rooms with cutaway walls, beams, candles, lanterns and fill light.
- **Shaders** (hand-written HLSL): Occluder (a URP Lit wrapper with the dithered holes), FX (soft particles and decals),
  Ghost (additive rim) and Sky (gradient, clouds, moon and stars).
- **`Atmosphere`** owns the sun, a three-colour ambient, exponential-squared fog, the sky, and the global post volume:
  ACES tonemapping, colour adjustments, bloom, vignette, film grain, white balance, shadows-midtones-highlights,
  lift-gamma-gain, and depth of field for dialogue. Seven presets: overcast day, night, dusk, mists, interior, one for
  the house at the edge of the fog, and dungeon.
- **`FX`** (379 lines): spell and combat effects (particles, projectiles, area blasts, flashes, status auras).
  **`GroundDecal`**: selection rings and area templates draped over the ground.
- **Highlights:** hovering tints the emission through a MaterialPropertyBlock (`_EmissionColor`).

## 7. Characters: how they're built and animated

### Building

- `ActorFactory.Spawn` turns a `Creature` into a visible, navigating actor according to its body kind: humanoids go to
  `HumanoidBuilder`, quadrupeds to `BeastBuilder`, and the swarm and one-off bodies to their own builders.
- **`HumanoidBuilder`** (772 lines) builds one skinned mesh per character from an `Appearance` (race, body type, build,
  height, colours, hair, beard, horns, face shape, tail, age and a few dress flags) and a `GearLook` (armour look, main
  and off hand, helmet, weapons drawn or sheathed).
  - **Skeleton:** 30 bones, created at runtime with no rotation: Root, Hips, Spine, Chest, Neck, Head; a clavicle,
    upper arm, forearm and hand on each side; a thigh, shin, foot and toe on each side; 4 tail bones; 4 cape bones.
  - **Mesh:** a lofted body with 13 material slots (skin, hair, eye white, iris, two cloth colours, leather, metal,
    trim, horn, dark, cape, face) and a painted face texture of 256 or 512 pixels.
  - **Sockets:** right hand, left hand, shield (on the forearm), back, left hip, right hip, head top, eyes.
  - **Weapons** (`WeaponBuilder`: 34 weapon and held-item looks, from daggers to a lute) are separate meshes that move
    between the hands (drawn) and the back or hip (sheathed).
  - **`HeadBuilder`** (921 lines): sculpted heads with eyes and lids, hair and beard shells, long hair and painted faces.
  - **`HumanoidArmor`**: plate as separate layered pieces, and jointed hands.
  - Changing equipment rebuilds the whole character (`ActorFactory.Rebuild`).
- **`BeastRig` and `BeastBuilder`** (512 lines): a 33-bone quadruped skeleton (a flexible back, a two-piece neck, jaw,
  ears, a five-bone tail, full front and hind legs), sculpted from anatomical landmarks and given clumps of fur.
- **Special bodies** (`CreatureBodies.cs`): a swarm of small flapping bodies and three one-off bodies, each with its own
  animator. The older `QuadrupedRig` and `QuadrupedAnimator` in that file are superseded by `BeastRig`.

### Animation

All of it is procedural. There's no Animator, no animation clip and no Mecanim anywhere.

- **One interface, `IAnimDriver`:** `Play(action, onImpact)`, `SetCombat`, `SetLife` (alive, downed, dead), `LookAt`,
  `SetTalking`, `Busy`, `Height`. Combat and dialogue only ever talk to this interface.
- **`HumanoidAnimator`** (867 lines) sets bone rotations every frame. It has a real gait with the feet planted by leg IK
  (heel-to-toe roll, pelvis bob and sway, counter-rotating shoulders), idles that shift weight, a combat stance for each
  grip (unarmed, one hand, shield, two hands, polearm, bow, crossbow), falls, look-at, talking, sitting and a cape on a
  spring. There are 8 motion styles: normal, noble, brute, child and four for monsters.
- **`HumanoidClips`** (208 lines): 24 keyed actions (slash, overhead, thrust, punch, bow, crossbow, throw, three casts,
  heal, drink, hit, dodge, cheer, interact, shove, breath, roar, claw, bite, bless, draw, stomp). Each is a few poses
  offset from the stance and joined by smooth curves, with an impact moment that combat waits for. Two-handed weapons
  are animated in the weapon's own space, with both hands placed on them by IK.
- **`BeastAnimator`** (453 lines): four-legged gaits (walk, trot, gallop) with paws planted by IK, a flexing spine, a
  springy tail and ears, bites, hits, howls, a crouched combat stance and falls.

## 9. What re-assetting would take

### 9.1 The import path

What the library exports today:

- **`<id>.npz`** is the source: voxel coordinates, a colour, a metal value and a glow value for every voxel.
- **`<id>.vox`** is lossy. Its palette is cut to 255 colours, but figures use 300 to 520 (the fighter 431, the guard
  519, the plain human man 309). It can't hold anything longer than 255 voxels (3.8 m), and it has no metal, glow or
  bones. Good for opening in MagicaVoxel; not a game format.
- **`model.glb`** (from Blender, `render_vox.py --glb`) has one quad per exposed voxel face, with four vertices of its
  own, a vertex colour and a flat normal. It has one plain rough material plus a material per glow colour. Metal is
  dropped. There's no skeleton and no LOD, and figures still stand on their review ground disc.
- **Sizes** (all 438 GLBs outside the sealed folder): dressed figures 40,000 to 75,000 triangles (median about 55,000,
  with twice as many vertices, 4 to 5 MB); bare race bodies about 34,000; the largest model 164,000; weapons 300 to
  10,000; armour and clothing items about 23,000 to 34,000 (median).

What the game needs is **a game export written by Python straight from the `.npz`**, with no Blender (the plan allows
one Blender at a time and agents never run it). It should give:

1. the mesh, merged and grouped by bone (numbers below), with voxel ambient occlusion baked into the vertices and the
   metal and glow values kept in a second vertex attribute;
2. for figures, a skin (joints and weights) on a skeleton that maps onto v2's bones (section 7; agent B's naming table
   has a column for v2);
3. nodes for sockets (hands, back, hips, head top) and each item's grip point;
4. one or two LODs made by halving the voxel resolution;
5. no ground disc, the pivot at the feet, and simple box colliders for props and buildings.

In Unity, glTFast (`com.unity.cloud.gltfast`, an official package) imports GLB in the editor with vertex colours and
skins. URP's Lit shader ignores vertex colours, so v3 needs a small voxel shader (9.3).

Measured on four library figures, with the same face rule as the GLB export:

| Model | Voxels | Triangles now | Same-colour faces merged | Merged with a colour atlas | Half-resolution LOD (colours merged) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Fighter (hero) | 49,544 | 59,996 | 49,570 | 17,910 | 10,796 |
| Guard (archetype) | 49,305 | 55,608 | 49,026 | 12,966 | 11,236 |
| Human man (race body) | 38,489 | 39,144 | 34,290 | 9,826 | 8,160 |
| Commoner (archetype) | 40,803 | 41,608 | 36,926 | 10,618 | 8,838 |

- Merging same-colour faces saves only 12 to 17%, because the kit's shading breaks up runs of one colour.
- Merging into boxes that carry a small pixel texture (an atlas; this is how Hytale's box models work) cuts the
  triangles by 3 to 4.5 times.
- A half-resolution LOD cuts them by about 5 times.
- On a skinned figure, merged faces must stay inside one bone's voxels.

### 9.2 Animation

- **v2's animators need no clips.** They need a skeleton that maps onto v2's bone set (`Hips`, `Spine`, `Chest`,
  `Neck`, `Head`, `ClavL`, `UpperArmL`…), the bind positions and the sockets. With that map, `HumanoidAnimator` and
  `HumanoidClips` can drive library figures almost unchanged; `HumanoidRig` would be filled from the imported bones
  instead of being built. Agent B's skeleton uses the Unity and Godot humanoid names and lists the v2 bone for each;
  its one extra bone, an upper chest, would take a share of v2's chest rotation.
- **The library's new posing module** (`kit\pose.py`, 2026-09-30) already gives each voxel one of 15 segments, with
  weights in eighths. How they map:

| v2 bones | Library segment |
|---|---|
| Hips, Spine, Chest | torso (the library bends between hips and chest; v2 has three bones there) |
| Neck, Head | neck, head |
| ClavL, ClavR | none (part of the torso) |
| UpperArm, LowerArm, Hand (each side) | uarm, farm, hand |
| Thigh, Shin, Foot (each side) | thigh, shin, foot |
| ToeL, ToeR | none (feet are one piece) |
| Tail0 to Tail3, Cape0 to Cape3 | none yet: tails, cloaks and capes need segments of their own |

- One bone per voxel (rigid skinning) keeps voxels crisp, and it's how Hytale animates: rigid parts on named bones, no
  weight painting. Elbows and knees need overlapping voxels; long skirts, capes and long hair need their own bones or
  blended weights. Agent B's report covers the rig.
- v2's rest pose has the arms 11° out from the body. Library figures are built in varied stances, some posed or holding
  props for their review stills. The game needs rest-pose builds, with held things as separate items on the sockets.
- Beasts: v2's 33-bone `BeastRig` is the target for C01's beasts.

### 9.3 Shaders and lighting for the pixel-art look

**The key number:** at v2's default camera (14 m away, 38° field of view), one 1.5 cm voxel covers about 1.1 screen
pixels at 720p, 1.7 at 1080p and 2.2 at 1440p. Zoomed in to 3.5 m it covers about 6.7 pixels at 1080p; zoomed out to
28 m, under one. So the library's voxels already sit at pixel scale in the default view: the figures will read as crisp
pixel art, and the blocks show as you zoom in.

What v3 needs:

1. **A voxel material** (a URP Shader Graph or HLSL shader): vertex colour as albedo, the baked voxel AO, a metal mask
   (the library's renderer scales metal by 0.6 and makes it shinier), and glow from a mask into HDR emission so bloom
   picks it up. It must keep v2's hooks: `_EmissionColor` for hover highlights, a dithered occluder version (today's
   occluder shader wraps URP Lit) and the ghost swap.
2. **Anti-aliasing:** keep MSAA 4×, which treats voxel edges as geometry. Test TAA and upscalers before using them;
   they can smear detail that is one or two pixels wide.
3. **LOD:** switch to the half-resolution model when zoomed out, or voxels smaller than a pixel will shimmer.
4. **Shadows:** a voxel is smaller than a shadow texel, so expect to tune bias and cascades. SSAO plus the baked voxel
   AO give the forms their depth under Barovia's overcast daylight.
5. **Optional:** one-pixel outlines from depth and normals, which help faceless figures read at a distance. A
   low-resolution "pixelate" pass only makes sense close up: at the default distance it would throw library detail
   away (a 640×360 target gives about 0.6 pixels per voxel).
6. **Retune the seven atmosphere presets** for flat-coloured voxels. They already match the two light registers:
   overcast daylight, and gothic night and interiors.
7. **Fog:** v2 uses exponential-squared fog plus particle fog banks. URP has no built-in volumetric fog; it would take
   a third-party or custom pass.

### 9.4 Character creation and equipment

- **v2 builds any body in any outfit at runtime from sliders. The library builds offline in Python and ships finished
  models.** So the creator has to become a choice among library bodies (race, sex and build) and library parts.
- **Equipment** needs one of two approaches. Either every combination is pre-built, or each worn piece is exported as a
  voxel layer fitted to each body, which the game stacks in paint order (as the kit does) and meshes at runtime. The
  second keeps BG3-style visible armour changes without building every combination. Porting the kit's Python painter to
  C# isn't realistic: the kit is over 400 KB of Python.
- **Races:** v2 offers 12, including drow, half-orc, githyanki and firbolg. The library has dragonborn, dwarf, elf,
  gnome, half-elf, halfling, human and tiefling today, with others planned or cut. The list for v3 is Peter's call.
- **The premade party:** v2's Arkus, Chairn and Dulandir are pilots. The library's Arkus is Peter's own and stays out of
  any game until he implements him himself. Chai'rn is locked in the library, and Dulandir has no brief yet.
- **Faceless:** v2's eyes, irises, glowing eyes, painted faces and face shapes all go. Head turns (`LookAt`) stay.

### 9.5 Environments

- At 1.5 cm, one face of a 10 m by 3 m wall is 133,000 voxel faces (266,000 triangles) before merging, and a village
  of 20 buildings would run to tens of millions. Environments need either a coarser voxel (agent E's study) or merging
  into textured boxes, plus simple box colliders. v2 bakes its navmesh from colliders, and its occluder rasterises
  every building's triangles.
- **Terrain:** v2 uses a Unity Terrain with painted layers. It could stay, with pixel-art layers, or become voxel chunks
  (batch E02).
- **The seam is `Kit.Place(key, factory)`.** Every prop already goes through a cache key, so a table from v2's keys to
  library ids lets each area switch piece by piece.

## 10. Risks, traps and loose ends

- **Static state.** Singletons and caches in static fields (`Game.I`, `MatLib`, `Kit`, `ProcTex`, the theme fonts,
  `AreaLoader`, `Dialogues`, the campaign's "registered" flag) rely on the domain reload. Unity 6.6 turns that reload off
  by default for new projects, and the CoreCLR editor (the Unity 6.8 alpha, then Unity 7) removes it. Unity's new
  lifecycle tools (in the 6.7 manual) include an `[AutoStaticsCleanup]` attribute for this. Keep v2's setting in the
  fork, and clean up the statics before moving past 6.7.
- **Build traps already solved** (keep them):
  - runtime-only materials need templates in `Resources\Mat` so their URP variants survive a build;
  - custom shaders must `multi_compile` their keywords;
  - terrain must not draw instanced;
  - URP post-processing stripping must stay off;
  - with the GPU Resident Drawer, a custom fragment wrapper must call `UNITY_SETUP_INSTANCE_ID` first;
  - moved renderers update only once per frame.
- **Tooling:** eval in the editor has a five-second limit on the main thread, and the whole dev loop depends on an
  experimental package (`com.unity.pipeline` 0.7.0-exp.1). Pin its version.
- **Fonts come from Windows.** A web build, or any other system, would need fonts shipped with the game.
- **No networking:** one player controls the whole party.
- **No tests, and no commits** in v2's own git repo. Back it up before forking.
- **Dead code:** `QuadrupedRig` and `QuadrupedAnimator` (replaced by `BeastRig`), one unused body kind, and the unused
  uGUI, Timeline and test packages.
- **Disk:** Library 1.5 GB, the captures 485 MB, the build 109 MB. Leave them out of the fork; Library regenerates.
- **`Plans\PROMPT_CH2_TO_END.md`:** its section 5 outlines the later chapters. Skip it to stay unspoiled.
- **Peter's v2 playtest notes** (from that file's section 4), to carry forward: later towns must feel populated
  (crowds with barks and routines); nothing may pop out of view; the minimap shows the camera's view; portraits have
  name plates; hair must not clip through heads.
