The look test

Dungine.v3, 3 October 2026. The first time the library's voxel art stands in the game.

Two choices for you:

  1. The look: (a) crisp voxels at full resolution, or (b) a pixelated render, at half or a third of the screen's resolution?
  2. Outlines (a thin dark line round each shape) or none?

Pictures for both are below. My suggestion: (a) with outlines. The library's detail survives, and the outline helps the figures stand out from busy ground.

What's in the game now

Eight library figures (the fighter hero, three village people from review 9, two Barovian commoners, a Barovian noble and a guard) and seven props (a barrel, a crate, a keg, a lamp post, a lantern, a campfire and a draft horse). They stand in the village square and beside the party where a new game starts. They're still statues: animation comes with the rigs, next. Everything else, the party included, is still v2's art.

The round display base each figure stands on in its review pictures is taken off when the model comes into the game.

Comparing the looks

Same view, same moment, at the usual camera distance. (b) draws the 3D scene at a lower resolution and enlarges it without smoothing, so every pixel becomes a hard square. The interface stays sharp either way.

  • (a) Full resolution: crisp. At the usual distance one voxel is about one and a half screen pixels, so the figures already read as pixel art, and zooming in shows the blocks.
  • (b) Half resolution: chunkier, still readable. A third: faces and small details merge, and the ground turns grainy.
  • Moving camera: stills can't show it, but a pixelated render shimmers as the camera pans unless the camera is locked to the pixel grid. That's extra work if you pick (b).
  • Outlines are found from depth, so they only appear where a shape stands in front of something.

Close up and far away

Close up you'll see thin dark streaks down the sides of faces and sleeves. They're the small sides of each voxel step, turned away from the light; they aren't shadows. The library's renders hide them with soft fill light. I'll fix them when the lighting is tuned.
